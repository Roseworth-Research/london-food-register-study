# Interpretation of administrative records

This research analyses administrative records and does not assess the conduct or
integrity of individual companies, directors, advisers, insolvency practitioners
or registered-office providers.

A registered office is a legal correspondence address and need not represent a
company's trading premises. Use of a professional or third-party registered
office, a change of registered office, strike-off, absence of filed accounts or
an insolvency-related filing is not treated by this study as evidence of fraud,
misconduct, concealment, tax avoidance or non-trading.

Address, company and exit classifications used in the analysis — for example
"service address", "single-company address", "pre-deadline exit", "registrar
strike-off" or "premises-confirmed" — are analytical classifications only. They
should not be interpreted as findings concerning the motives or conduct of any
individual business or person.

## The VAT analysis

The VAT analysis models the economic effect of published VAT rules using public
information. It does not estimate or make findings concerning any named
company's actual VAT liability, tax compliance, tax planning, motives or
conduct.

Every figure about a named operator is one of three kinds, and is labelled as
such in Paper 2:

| Kind | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Disclosed fact** | Taken directly from filed accounts or another primary source | Greggs plc revenue, FY2025: £2,151.2m |
| **Model assumption** | A value or classification introduced by the researchers | The share of Greggs' hot-food sales kept hot |
| **Model output** | A figure computed from disclosed facts plus assumptions | Greggs keeps £8.82–£9.34 of every £10 of consumer spending |

A model output is never a company's actual VAT position.

## What this study does not establish

The study does not establish whether an individual company traded, whether a
physical premises opened or closed, why a company was struck off, why its
registered office changed, whether an individual business complied with tax
law, whether a company engaged in tax planning, or whether any person or
organisation acted improperly. It does not establish any causal effect of VAT
on the formation, survival or location of food businesses.
