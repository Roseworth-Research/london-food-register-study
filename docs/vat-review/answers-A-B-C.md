# VAT worksheet — answers A1 to C2

Checked against primary sources on 1 October 2026. Short extracts only.
Companion file for D1–H3: `answers-D-E-F-H.md`. Status of all twenty: `STATUS.md`.

---

**A1 — Yes.** Food supplied in the course of catering is excluded from zero-rating, and
catering includes "any supply of it for consumption on the premises on which it is
supplied". Notice 709/1 §2.2: "Any supply of food or drink as part of a contract for
catering is standard-rated"; eat-in is standard-rated whether the food is hot or cold.

- Sources: VATA 1994 Sch 8 Grp 1, general item 1 and Note (3) —
  https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1994/23/schedule/8 ; HMRC Notice 709/1 §2.2 —
  https://www.gov.uk/guidance/catering-takeaway-food-and-vat-notice-7091
- Consequence: model assumption (eat-in 100% standard-rated) stands.

**A2 — Yes.** Notice 709/1 §3.2: premises include "any area set aside for the
consumption of food by the food retailers' customers, whether or not the area may also
be used by the customers of other food retailers" — seating in shops, adjacent outdoor
tables, shared food courts and pavement chairs. General public seating not designated
for a retailer's customers is excluded.

- Source: Notice 709/1 §3.2 (URL above).
- Consequence: Paper 2's wording on pavement tables and food courts stands; the
  eat-in/takeaway split is described with that caveat.

**B1 — Yes.** Note (3B) and Notice 709/1 §4.2: food is hot if it is above ambient
temperature when supplied AND meets any one of: heated for the purposes of enabling it
to be consumed hot; heated to order; kept hot after being heated; supplied in packaging
that retains heat or is designed for hot food; advertised or marketed as supplied hot.
Only one test need be met.

- Sources: VATA 1994 Sch 8 Grp 1 Notes (3B)–(3C); Notice 709/1 §4.2.
- Consequence: model assumption (any one test met = standard-rated) stands.

**B2 — Yes.** Notice 709/1 §4.4: "freshly baked bread or bakery products that are
incidentally hot at the time they're sold but which are often eaten cold" are
zero-rated if no test is met; HMRC's Example 1 is a Cornish pasty cooling naturally
with no heat retention — zero-rated. *Sub One Ltd v HMRC* [2014] EWCA Civ 773 held
heated-to-order toasted subs standard-rated and confirmed an objective purpose test; it
concerns heating to order and does not narrow the cooling exclusion.

- Sources: Notice 709/1 §4.4; https://caselaw.nationalarchives.gov.uk/ewca/civ/2014/773
- Consequence: model "retail bake, cooling = zero-rated" stands.

**B3 — Yes.** Notice 709/1 §4.2–4.3 names toasted bread, sandwiches and panini (heated
to order), rotisserie chickens (marketed hot), and items kept hot in a heated cabinet or
in water — all standard-rated.

- Source: Notice 709/1 §4.2–4.3.
- Consequence: stands.

**B4 — Partly.** Citable: in 2012 Greggs plc publicly opposed VAT on freshly baked
savouries "where there is no attempt to keep it hot and which is not designed to be kept
hot" (chairman's interim statement, as reported in the trade press), and the May 2012
revision exempted goods cooling naturally. Later in 2012 Greggs began selling more food
kept hot. No current public statement gives its kept-hot share.

- Sources: https://www.foodmanufacture.co.uk/Article/2012/05/29/Chancellor-takes-Greggs-advice-on-pasty-tax/ ;
  https://www.thegrocer.co.uk/news/after-pastygate-greggs-makes-u-turn-to-heat-up-more-food/236667.article
  (trade press reporting Greggs' own statements; no primary Greggs document located).
- Consequence: Greggs is printed as the £8.82–£9.34 range, never a point; rack-cooling
  is described as what Greggs "can" do.

**C1 — Yes.** Notice 709/1 §4.1: "Hot takeaway drinks are standard-rated." The
temporary reduced rate (15 July 2020 – 31 March 2022) covered "hot takeaway food and hot
takeaway non-alcoholic drinks" and food and non-alcoholic drinks consumed on the
premises; alcohol was excluded.

- Sources: Notice 709/1 §4.1;
  https://www.gov.uk/guidance/vat-reduced-rate-for-hospitality-holiday-accommodation-and-attractions
- Consequence: model treatment of hot drinks in the relief window stands; "always
  standard-rated" sentences are scoped outside that window.

**C2 — Yes.** Beverages are excepted item 4 (standard-rated): carbonated drinks,
cordials, fruit juice, bottled water. Overriding items restore zero-rating to tea
(item 4), cocoa, coffee and their preparations (item 5), and milk and its preparations
(item 6). Notice 701/14 §3.7.2: "Milk and flavoured milk drinks (including milk shakes)"
are zero-rated, with no milk-content threshold stated; cold zero-rated drinks supplied
for off-premises consumption "can be treated as zero-rated"; all hot beverages and all
drinks consumed on the premises are standard-rated.

- Source: https://www.gov.uk/guidance/food-products-and-vat-notice-70114
- Consequence: Tree D's "generally zero-rated" for cold milk drinks tightened to
  "zero-rated"; the 22% standard-rated attachment in the cold-takeaway basket remains a
  stated assumption.
