# VAT research answers: questions D, E, F, H

Prepared for the Roseworth research paper (Bricked-Up Windows). All sources checked 1 October 2026. Primary sources only (legislation.gov.uk, gov.uk HMRC notices and internal manuals, Companies House filings, platforms’ own published terms). Quotations are verbatim and short.

Headline flags:
- **D1 changes the model.** Cold ready-to-drink coffee sold for off-premises consumption is zero-rated as a “preparation” of coffee, not standard-rated. The 100% standard-rated assumption for RTD coffee must be changed.
- **E2** is answered from Costa Express Limited’s own filed accounts: the company describes itself as selling the drinks, which points to Costa (not the site partner) as seller. Individual site contracts are not public, so present this as “public filings indicate”, not as settled VAT fact.
- **F1(b) Just Eat** consumer and restaurant terms could not be read on 1 October 2026 (the site served a bot-check page, which was not bypassed). Only Just Eat for Business terms were verified.
- **Context not asked for but relevant:** HMRC guidance (updated 8 June 2026) records a further temporary 5% reduced rate for certain children’s meals and family attractions from 25 June 2026 to 1 September 2026 (Revenue and Customs Brief 5 (2026)). If the paper discusses current hospitality rates, it should mention this.

## D1. Retail coffee (beans, ground, pods) and ready-to-drink coffee

**Answer: Partly.** Beans, ground coffee and instant coffee are zero-rated under overriding item 5. Pods and capsules are zero-rated in principle, because the contents are coffee and normal packaging takes the liability of what it contains, but no HMRC text names pods. **Ready-to-drink (RTD) coffee is NOT always standard-rated.** HMRC says coffee-based beverages are zero-rated as preparations of coffee. Standard rating applies only when the drink is sold hot or for consumption on the premises (catering).

**Source extracts:**
- VATA 1994 Sch 8 Grp 1, items overriding the exceptions, item 5: “Cocoa, coffee and chicory and other roasted coffee substitutes, and preparations and extracts thereof.”
- VFOOD7720: “Zero-rating therefore extends to coffee beans, instant coffee, drinking chocolate and cocoa, chicory”. It also names “coffee or cocoa based beverages” as qualifying.
- Notice 701/14 para 3.7.2: “All hot beverages and any drinks, including zero-rated drinks sold for consumption on your premises are standard-rated.” The same paragraph says cold zero-rated drinks sold for off-premises consumption “can be treated as zero-rated”.
- Packaging: VFOOD2820: “if a supply of the contents is zero-rated, zero-rating also applies to the packaging.”

**Citations:**
- VATA 1994 Sch 8 Group 1: https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1994/23/schedule/8
- HMRC VFOOD7720 (page updated 24 July 2024): https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/vat-food/vfood7720
- VFOOD7540: https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/vat-food/vfood7540
- VFOOD2820: https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/vat-food/vfood2820
- VAT Notice 701/14, paras 2.2, 3.7.2, 6.3 (last updated 8 June 2026): https://www.gov.uk/guidance/food-products-and-vat-notice-70114
- All checked 1 October 2026.

**Consequence:**
- Grocery coffee at 0%: **stands** (beans, ground, instant; pods by inference, flagged as such).
- RTD coffee at 100% standard-rated: **must change.** Use 0% for cold canned or bottled coffee sold off-premises. Use 20% only when it is sold hot, consumed on the premises, or the drink is really a soft drink with coffee as only one of several ingredients. Notice 701/14 applies that last exclusion expressly to tea drinks; applying it to coffee is our analogy, not HMRC text.

**Pods: Unclear on direct authority.** No HMRC text found that mentions pods or capsules.

## D2. Wholesale bread, bakery and roasted coffee to trade customers

**Answer: Yes, with exceptions.** Selling food to a business that will itself sell it in the course of catering is not catering. HMRC treats catering as a supply to the final consumer. Bread, cakes and roasted coffee sold wholesale are therefore zero-rated. Excepted items stay standard-rated at every stage, for example chocolate-covered biscuits, confectionery, crisps and soft drinks. Food delivered hot so that it can be eaten hot is catering (P and S Catering).

**Source extracts:**
- VFOOD5220: “A supply in the course of catering is, for VAT purposes, to be taken as a supply to the final consumer”.
- VFOOD5220: “Where a trader supplies prepared cold food and drink to a customer who then sells it in the course of catering, the first supply is not regarded as being made in the course of catering.”
- Notice 709/1 para 2.2.1: food the customer must prepare (thaw, cook, reheat, plate) “is not a supply in the course of catering”.

**Citations:**
- HMRC VFOOD5220: https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/vat-food/vfood5220
- VAT Notice 709/1, paras 2.1 and 2.2.1 (last updated 8 June 2026): https://www.gov.uk/guidance/catering-takeaway-food-and-vat-notice-7091
- Checked 1 October 2026.

**Consequence:** Wholesale food to trade at 0% **stands**, provided the wholesale mix excludes excepted items and hot food sent out to be eaten hot. If wholesale lines include confectionery, chocolate biscuits, crisps or soft drinks, carve those out at 20%.

## D3. Chilled and frozen supermarket lines: about 5% standard-rated residue

**Answer: Unclear (as a number). The characterisation is plausible.** The excepted items show which supermarket lines are standard-rated: ice cream and similar frozen products, confectionery, crisps and savoury snacks, roasted or salted nuts, most non-milk, non-tea, non-coffee beverages including bottled water, sports drinks, alcohol and home-brew products. A chilled or frozen food range is mostly zero-rated meals, meat, dairy, vegetables and desserts. The obvious standard-rated parts are ice cream, frozen confectionery and any chilled soft drinks. No primary source gives a percentage.

**Source extract:** Notice 701/14 para 2.2 lists as always standard-rated: “Ice cream, similar products, and mixes for making them”, “Confectionery, apart from cakes and some biscuits”, “Other beverages”, and “Potato crisps, roasted or salted nuts and some other savoury snack products”.

**Citations:**
- VAT Notice 701/14 para 2.2: https://www.gov.uk/guidance/food-products-and-vat-notice-70114
- VATA 1994 Sch 8 Grp 1, excepted items 1 to 7: https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1994/23/schedule/8
- Checked 1 October 2026.

**Consequence:** Keep the 5% figure only if it is stated as a practice assumption. Suggested wording: “the share of standard-rated lines is a practice assumption; HMRC publishes no breakdown”. A frozen range that is heavy in ice cream would carry a higher residue.

## E1. Franchise royalties standard-rated; franchisee fully taxable

**Answer: Yes.**
- **Royalties:** no exemption or zero-rating covers franchise fees, so they are standard-rated when the franchisor is UK-based. This follows from the general scheme of the Act, not from an HMRC text that names franchise royalties.
- **Franchisee recovery:** zero-rated supplies are taxable supplies. A franchisee making only standard-rated and zero-rated supplies is not partly exempt and can recover input tax in full. The normal blocks still apply, for example business entertainment and cars.

**Source extracts:**
- Notice 700 para 4.1: “you can reclaim from HMRC as much of the VAT on your purchases and imports, as relates to the standard-rated, reduced-rated and zero-rated supplies you make.”
- Notice 700 para 3.5: zero-rated supplies “are treated as taxable supplies in all other respects, including the right … to recover the VAT on their own business expenditure”.
- Notice 706 para 1.1: “A business is partly exempt if it makes, or intends to make, both taxable and exempt supplies”.

**Citations:**
- VAT Notice 700, paras 3.2, 3.5, 4.1 (last updated 25 June 2026): https://www.gov.uk/guidance/vat-guide-notice-700
- VAT Notice 706, para 1.1 (last updated 16 December 2025): https://www.gov.uk/guidance/partial-exemption-vat-notice-706
- Checked 1 October 2026.

**Consequence:** The assumption **stands**. In the paper, describe royalty standard rating as following from the general rules, not from a specific HMRC statement.

## E2. Costa Express: who sells the cup?

**Answer: Partly. Public filings indicate Costa (Costa Express Limited) is the seller.**
- Costa Express Limited’s audited accounts give its principal activity as selling drinks from its machines.
- It recognises revenue at the price the consumer pays when the consumer receives the drink. That is the treatment of a principal, not an agent.
- FY2024 revenue was £367.8m across 16,873 machines.
- Secondary trade descriptions say site partners take a revenue share. That fits Costa selling and the site being paid a fee or commission.

**Limits:** the site-partner contracts are not public, and the accounts do not state the VAT treatment. Accounting principal status points strongly to the VAT position but does not prove it.

**Source extracts (Costa Express Limited, Annual Report and Financial Statements, year ended 31 December 2024):**
- Strategic Report p.2: “The principal activity of the Company during the year was the sale of espresso based coffee and other beverages from branded self-serve machines.”
- Note 2, Revenue recognition, p.23: “revenue is recognised at this point at the price for the items purchased”, meaning when “the customer receives the drink”.

**Citations:**
- Companies House, Costa Express Limited (03145187), full accounts made up to 31 December 2024, filing history: https://find-and-update.company-information.service.gov.uk/company/03145187/filing-history
- Checked 1 October 2026.

**Consequence:** Treat Costa as the seller of the cup, with the site partner earning a fee or commission. Hot drinks sold this way are standard-rated (VFOOD5160 and Notice 709/1 para 4.2). State that this rests on Costa’s filed accounts, that individual site contracts are not public, and that some sites could be contracted differently.

## E3. Repayment-trader mechanics

**Answer: Yes.** When input VAT that can be claimed is more than output VAT due, the return is a repayment return and HMRC repays the difference. A business that normally expects repayments can ask for monthly VAT periods. Separately, a business whose supplies are all or mostly zero-rated may apply for exemption from registration. If HMRC grants that exemption, the business cannot reclaim input tax.

**Source extracts:**
- Notice 700/12 para 2.4: “If the input VAT you can claim is greater than the output VAT you owe, you’ll be due a repayment of VAT from us. This is a repayment return.”
- Notice 700 para 20.3.2: “You can ask for monthly tax periods if you normally expect to receive repayments.”

**Citations:**
- VAT Notice 700/12, para 2.4 (last updated 19 December 2025): https://www.gov.uk/guidance/how-to-fill-in-and-submit-your-vat-return-vat-notice-70012
- VAT Notice 700, para 20.3.2 (last updated 25 June 2026): https://www.gov.uk/guidance/vat-guide-notice-700
- VAT Notice 700/1, para 3.11: https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/vat-notice-7001-should-i-be-registered-for-vat/vat-notice-7001-should-i-be-registered-for-vat
- Checked 1 October 2026.

**Consequence:** **Stands.** Suggested neutral wording: “a business whose input tax exceeds its output tax receives a repayment and may request monthly returns.”

## E4. Input-side assumptions

**(a) Rent bears VAT only if the landlord has opted to tax: Yes**, with two caveats. Lettings of land and buildings are normally exempt. An option to tax makes them standard-rated, though some supplies remain exempt even after an option (Notice 742A section 3). Ancillary items such as service charges usually follow the rent.
- Notice 742A para 1.2: “Supplies of land and buildings, such as freehold sales, leasing or renting, are normally exempt from VAT.” Once you have opted, supplies “will normally be standard-rated”.
- Citation: VAT Notice 742A para 1.2 (last updated 26 May 2026): https://www.gov.uk/guidance/opting-to-tax-land-and-buildings-notice-742a. Checked 1 October 2026.

**(b) Packaging bought by a business is standard-rated: Yes, by default.**
- Empty packaging (cups, lids, boxes, bags) is not food and is not listed in Sch 8 or Sch 9, so the standard rate applies.
- Packaging is zero-rated only when it is sold as part of zero-rated food it contains (Notice 701/14 para 6.3; Notice 700 para 8.2).
- No HMRC text says in terms that “empty packaging is standard-rated”; this follows from the general rules.
- Notice 701/14 para 6.3: packaging “simply to contain, protect and promote the food it contains, is considered part of the supply of the food inside it”.
- Citation: https://www.gov.uk/guidance/food-products-and-vat-notice-70114. Checked 1 October 2026.

**(c) Wages and depreciation are outside the scope of VAT: Yes.**
- VATSC03540: “the provision of services by an employee to his or her employer is outside the scope of VAT.”
- Depreciation is an accounting entry, not a supply, so it carries no VAT. VAT on the underlying asset is reclaimed when the asset is bought, subject to the normal rules, not over its life.
- Citation: HMRC VATSC03540: https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/vat-supply-and-consideration/vatsc03540. Checked 1 October 2026.

**Quantities:** “55% of property costs bearing VAT” and “costs default to 93% of revenue” are **practice assumptions**. No primary source gives either figure. Label them as assumptions in the paper.

**Consequence:** (a), (b) and (c) **stand**. The model should treat asset VAT as recovered when the asset is bought, not spread over its life through depreciation.

## F1. Delivery platforms (Deliveroo, Uber Eats, Just Eat)

**(a) Commission and service fees are standard-rated services: Yes.** Uber Eats and Deliveroo both publish terms charging their fees plus VAT. These are UK business-to-business services with no exemption.
- Uber Eats Merchant Terms (GB): “All Fees payable pursuant to this Agreement are exclusive of VAT.”
- Deliveroo Partner Policies (Cancellations policy): the partner is charged “their standard commission fee to Deliveroo (plus VAT/GST where applicable)”.

**(b) The restaurant is the principal seller and the platform is its agent: Yes for Deliveroo and Uber Eats. Unclear for Just Eat.**
- **Deliveroo (Roofoods Ltd), Terms of Service, last updated 9 July 2026:** “When you order from a Partner, you are buying the Items from one of our Partners and Deliveroo acts as an agent on behalf of that Partner”. Also: “Payment is made directly to Deliveroo acting as agent on behalf of the Partner only.”
  - https://deliveroo.co.uk/legal
  - Partner Policies, Cancellations policy last updated 14 July 2026: https://merchants.deliveroo.com/legal/policies
- **Uber Eats (Uber Eats UK Limited, reg. 10078453), Merchant Terms and Conditions, last modified 16 June 2025:** “Merchant is the ‘retailer’ or ‘seller’ of all Items”. Uber UK is appointed as the merchant’s “limited payment collection agent”.
  - https://www.uber.com/legal/en/document/?country=great-britain&lang=en-gb&name=uber-eats-merchant-terms-and-conditions
- **Just Eat:** the consumer terms at https://www.just-eat.co.uk/terms-and-conditions returned a Cloudflare bot-check page on 1 October 2026. It was not bypassed, so the terms were not read. The full restaurant agreement is not published (promotion terms refer only to a “standard restaurant agreement”).
  - The group’s Just Eat for Business terms (City Pantry Ltd) say: “The legal contract for the supply and purchase of Products is between you and the Business … we will facilitate and conclude the sale of Products on behalf of, and as agent for, the Businesses in all cases.”
  - https://business.just-eat.co.uk/customer-terms
  - This supports the same model but is not the consumer marketplace contract. A human should check the Just Eat consumer terms in a browser.

**(c) Delivered hot food is standard-rated as catering: Yes.**
- Notice 709/1 para 2.1 lists among obvious catering supplies: “delivery of cooked ready-to-eat food or meals (with or without crockery or cutlery)”.
- Para 4.2: food that is hot when provided and meets any of five tests is standard-rated.
- Cold items such as cold desserts or sealed groceries follow their own liability (paras 2.2.2 and 2.2.3), and excepted items such as soft drinks are standard-rated anyway.
- Citation: VAT Notice 709/1 (last updated 8 June 2026): https://www.gov.uk/guidance/catering-takeaway-food-and-vat-notice-7091

All terms checked 1 October 2026.

**Consequence:** The model (restaurant is principal, output VAT on the gross food price, commission is a standard-rated recoverable input) **stands** for Deliveroo and Uber Eats. For Just Eat, flag it as “believed to be the same; consumer terms not verified on the check date”.

## F2. Registration thresholds and tests

**Answer: Yes on all points.**
- **Thresholds:** registration £90,000 and deregistration £88,000, both from 1 April 2024. They were previously £85,000 and £83,000.
- **Registration tests:** (i) at the end of any month, taxable supplies in the previous 12 months are over the threshold (rolling); (ii) at any time, taxable supplies in the next 30 days alone are expected to go over the threshold.
- **Unregistered businesses:** a business that is not registered cannot issue VAT invoices or charge VAT, and cannot reclaim input VAT.

**Source extracts:**
- Policy paper: “The VAT registration and deregistration thresholds will increase from 1 April 2024.”
- Notice 700/1 para 3.3: liable to register if “at the end of any month, the value of your taxable supplies in the previous 12 months or less is over the registration threshold”, or if you expect supplies “in the next 30 day period alone, to go over the registration threshold”.
- Notice 700 para 19.5.1: “Only a VAT-registered person can issue a VAT invoice.”
- Notice 700/12 para 2.4: input VAT is reclaimable “if you’re registered for VAT”.

**Citations:**
- HMRC policy paper, Increasing the VAT registration threshold: https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/vat-increasing-the-registration-and-deregistration-thresholds/increasing-the-vat-registration-threshold
- gov.uk, VAT thresholds: https://www.gov.uk/how-vat-works/vat-thresholds (registration “More than £90,000”; deregistration “Less than £88,000”)
- VAT Notice 700/1 para 3.3: https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/vat-notice-7001-should-i-be-registered-for-vat/vat-notice-7001-should-i-be-registered-for-vat
- VAT Notice 700 para 19.5.1
- All checked 1 October 2026.

**Consequence:** **Stands.**

## H1. Temporary reduced rate for hospitality, 2020 to 2022

**Answer: Yes on every element.**
- **Rates and dates:** 5% from 15 July 2020 to 30 September 2021; 12.5% from 1 October 2021 to 31 March 2022; standard rate again from 1 April 2022.
- **Scope:** food and non-alcoholic drink for consumption on the premises, plus hot takeaway food and hot takeaway non-alcoholic drinks.
- **Exclusion:** alcoholic beverages were excluded.
- **Cold takeaway:** cold takeaway food was not covered because it was already zero-rated, not catering. The Order covers only supplies “in the course of catering” and Notice 709/1 para 4.1 confirms cold takeaway is zero-rated.

**Source extracts:**
- SI 2020/728, inserted Group 14, item 1: “Supplies in the course of catering of— (a) any food or drink for consumption on the premises … (b) any hot food or hot drink for consumption off those premises, except supplies of alcoholic beverages.” The Order came into force on 15 July 2020.
- Finance Act 2021 s.93, “Temporary 12.5% reduced rate for hospitality and tourism sectors”: “5 per cent” is to be read as “12.5 per cent” for the period ending 31 March 2022.
- HMRC guidance: “5% from 15 July 2020 to 30 September 2021”; “12.5% from 1 October 2021 to 31 March 2022”; “From 1 April 2022 the normal VAT rules apply”.

**Citations:**
- The Value Added Tax (Reduced Rate) (Hospitality and Tourism) (Coronavirus) Order 2020, SI 2020/728: https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2020/728/made
- Finance Act 2021 s.93: https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2021/26/section/93
- HMRC, VAT: reduced rate for hospitality, holiday accommodation and attractions (last updated 23 December 2022): https://www.gov.uk/guidance/vat-reduced-rate-for-hospitality-holiday-accommodation-and-attractions
- All checked 1 October 2026.

**Consequence:** **Stands.**
- The original 5% end date of 12 January 2021 was extended twice: first to 31 March 2021, then to 30 September 2021 under FA 2021.
- Separate new measure: a 5% rate for certain children’s meals and family attractions applied from 25 June 2026 to 1 September 2026 (Revenue and Customs Brief 5 (2026), updated 27 July 2026: https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/revenue-and-customs-brief-5-2026-temporary-reduced-rate-of-vat-for-childrens-meals-tickets-and-family-attractions/temporary-reduced-rate-of-vat-for-childrens-meals-tickets-and-family-attractions). If the paper describes hospitality VAT “today”, add a sentence on this.

## H2. Rate history

**Answer: Yes on every date.**
- Hot takeaway food became standard-rated from 1 May 1984. Finance Act 1984 s.10 and Sch 6 Part I apply to supplies made on or after 1 May 1984.
- The standard rate was then 15%, in force since 18 June 1979.
- 17.5% from 1 April 1991.
- 15% from 1 December 2008.
- 17.5% from 1 January 2010. The 15% period therefore ran from 1 December 2008 to 31 December 2009.
- 20% from 4 January 2011.

**Source extracts:**
- VFOOD0240: “1st May 1984 Hot take-away food became standard-rated. Note (3) amended to expand the coverage of catering.”
- FA 1984 s.10 (as enacted), commencement: Part I of Sch 6 applies to supplies made on or after 1 May 1984.
- Notice 700 para 3.3.1, Historic VAT rates table: 18 June 1979 15%; 1 April 1991 17.5%; 1 December 2008 15%; 1 January 2010 17.5%; 4 January 2011 20%.

**Citations:**
- HMRC VFOOD0240: https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/vat-food/vfood0240
- VFOOD0180: https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/vat-food/vfood0180
- Finance Act 1984 s.10 (enacted): https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1984/43/section/10/enacted
- VAT Notice 700 para 3.3.1: https://www.gov.uk/guidance/vat-guide-notice-700
- All checked 1 October 2026.
- The House of Commons Library site refused automated access (HTTP 403), so Notice 700 is cited instead. It is an equally primary HMRC source.

**Consequence:** **Stands.**

## H3. Jaffa Cakes and the 2012 “pasty tax”

**Jaffa Cakes: Yes.**
- Suggested one-liner: “In United Biscuits (LON/91/0160) the VAT Tribunal held that Jaffa Cakes are cakes, not chocolate-covered biscuits, so they stay zero-rated.”
- Background: HMRC (then Customs and Excise) had decided Jaffa Cakes were biscuits partly covered in chocolate, and McVitie’s appealed.
- The year 1991 matches the LON/91 reference. VFOOD does not state the decision year separately.
- Source extract, VFOOD6260: “the tribunal thought they had enough characteristics of cakes to be accepted as such, and they were therefore zero-rated.”
- Citation: HMRC VFOOD6260: https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/vat-food/vfood6260. Checked 1 October 2026.

**Pasty tax: Yes.**
- Suggested one-liner: “Budget 2012 (21 March 2012) proposed taxing food sold above ambient temperature. After consultation, the Government announced revised tests on 28 May 2012 under which bakery items cooling naturally on the shelf stay zero-rated, while items kept hot are standard-rated (in force 1 October 2012).”
- Source extract, HMRC summary of responses, para 2.1.7: “the Government announced a revised set of tests on 28 May 2012 … Under the new tests food that is cooling naturally, such as Cornish pasties and sausage rolls, will continue to be zero-rated.”
- Para 2.1.9 adds that such items are “zero-rated where they are cooling naturally in the racks, but not when they are stored in heated cabinets”.
- Citations:
  - HMRC, VAT: Addressing borderline anomalies, Summary of responses, 28 June 2012 (Commons deposited paper DEP2012-1088): http://data.parliament.uk/DepositedPapers/Files/DEP2012-1088/summary-of-responses-VAT-anomalies.pdf
  - Consultation ran 21 March to 18 May 2012 (para 1.9).
  - The 1 October 2012 commencement is confirmed by VFOOD0240 and VFOOD4140, which record Notes (3A) to (3D) introduced from 1 October 2012.
  - All checked 1 October 2026.
- Wording note: the primary source says “cooling naturally in the racks” rather than “ambient shelves”. Use the HMRC wording.

**Consequence:** Both descriptions **stand**, with the wording adjusted as above.
